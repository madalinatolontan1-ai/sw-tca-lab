Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA): hardware and software DFMEA

What is FMEA?

FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis) is a risk analysis done before the failure exists. The team takes every element of the product, writes what it has to do (the functions), asks how it might fail to do it (the failure modes), what happens then (the effects) and why it could happen (the causes). For every effect – mode – cause chain it rates the risk and decides what to do so that the risk drops. FMEA is preventive: it is worth most at the start, when a change is cheap, and less with every phase in which the failure stays undiscovered.

The AIAG-VDA handbook (2019), used in the automotive industry, has three types of FMEA:

TypeWhat it analysesExample on the CAN module
DFMEA (design)The product design: the schematic, the components chosen, the layout, the code, the requirementsR4 chosen with too low a power rating; SENSOR_MAX written in another unit
PFMEA (process)The manufacturing and assembly processC2 missing after placement; cold solder joint on J2 (the HW TCA lab)
FMEA-MSRMonitoring and system response in operation, in the vehicleThe radar detects bus-off and reports the fault to the ADAS ECU

In this lab we do a DFMEA on the CAN node of the front radar, on two of its parts: a hardware signal and a software function. The method is the same for both. Only the causes differ: in hardware they are about choosing and dimensioning the components, the schematic and the layout; in software every cause is systematic (requirements, constants, logic, data types, interfaces), because software does not wear out and is not manufactured unit by unit.

Why, when and on what

  • Why: so that failures are found and removed in the design, not at the customer. A failure found in the field costs much more than one found in design (recalls, warranty, legal liability).
  • When: the DFMEA starts together with the design (concept, schematic, software architecture) and is updated at every change; it is a living document. The PFMEA starts when the production line is designed.
  • On what: on the system (the radar), on subsystems and components (the CAN node, the transceiver, the filters), on software (the functions) and on the manufacturing process.
  • Who: a team, not one person: HW designer, SW developer, test and validation engineer, functional safety engineer, quality engineer. In the lab, you play all these roles.

Standards, laws and industries

FieldWhat requires FMEA
AutomotiveThe AIAG-VDA FMEA handbook (2019) and SAE J1739 (2021) describe the method. IATF 16949 requires the design risk analysis (DFMEA) among the design outputs and the PFMEA among the process outputs. ISO 26262-9 (clause 8) requires safety analyses, and FMEA is the inductive analysis used most often.
GeneralIEC 60812:2018 describes FMEA and FMECA for any industry.
AerospaceSAE ARP4761A (2023) for the safety assessment; SAE AS13004 for PFMEA and the control plan.
MedicalISO 14971 for risk management of medical devices; FMEA is one of the techniques used.
RailwayEN 50126 (RAMS), with FMEA as a basic analysis.
TelecomThere is no dedicated FMEA standard. TL 9000 is the quality management system of the ICT sector; for the analysis IEC 60812 is used and, for reliability, Telcordia SR-332.

What happens if FMEA is not done: at the IATF 16949 audit, its absence is a nonconformity, and the supplier may lose the certification and the customer. If the failure reaches the field, recalls and product liability follow; the new European product liability directive (EU 2024/2853) explicitly includes software.

The case study and the app

We work on the same generic automotive front radar as in the TCA labs (lab assumption: ASIL B), which sends the list of detected objects over CAN at 500 kbit/s, about every 72 ms. The CAN node has the hardware part from the HW TCA lab (the module with the MCP2551 transceiver) and the software part from the SW TCA lab (the receive functions).

How you work: you work in the web app FMEA Lab, in the browser, with nothing to install. Enter your name and the variant code and press „Load variant”. In the lab you work on the demo variant F0, together with the instructor: the RC filters on CANH / CANL (HW) and the plausibility check of the azimuth angle, the function sensor_value_plausible() (SW). At home you do the same analysis on your own variant (F1–F6) and send the report. The app has a tab for the HW analysis, one for the SW analysis and the Report tab. Each analysis has three stages: steps 2–3 (structure and functions), step 4 (the failure net) and steps 5–6 (risk and optimization). At every stage you find „Context”, „How to do it” (with the example on F0) and the AIAG-VDA reference tables. Your work is saved in the browser you use.

Lab simplification: choose a single function per element, at most 3 failure modes, 3 effects and 2 causes per mode. The app warns you when you go past these limits, but does not block you. In industry the analysis covers every function and every failure; here we look at how you rate them, not at a complete analysis.

Structure and functions (steps 2–3)

The structure tree has three levels. N1 is the effect level: the radar, seen from the vehicle. N2 is the element analysed: the hardware signal or the software function of your variant; this is where the failure modes appear. N3 is the cause level: the design cause categories.

LevelHW analysisSW analysis
N1 (effect)The front radar (ASIL B)The front radar (ASIL B)
N2 (mode)The signal of the variant (e.g. the RC filters on CANH / CANL)The function of the variant (e.g. sensor_value_plausible() for the azimuth angle)
N3 (cause)Component selection, Component dimensioning, Signal definition, Schematic, PCB layoutSoftware requirements (SWR), Constants and parameters (#define), Decision logic, Data types and value ranges, Interface (calls, return values)

A good function has a verb, an object and a measurable condition, for example „Provides 120 Ω ±1 % between CANH and CANL at the end of the bus”. You write the N2 functions starting from the requirements shown (HWR for hardware, SWR for software). The suggestion list in the app is the same for every level: you decide where each function fits.

The failure net (step 4)

Failures are the negation of the functions: the function is missing, partial, intermittent, unwanted or out of limits. At N1 you write the effects (what the vehicle or the ADAS ECU sees), at N2 the failure modes of the element, at N3 the causes. Then link every mode to at least one effect and at least one cause. Severity is rated on the effect, and the mode takes the highest severity of the effects linked to it.

On every cause you choose the culprit: in HW, the component (those on the signal are highlighted on the board and in the schematic); in SW, the requirement, if the cause is in the requirements category. Mind the level: „C2 has too wide a tolerance” is a cause (N3), not a mode; the mode is what the filter does wrong (asymmetry between CANH and CANL).

Risk analysis (step 5)

For every mode – cause link you add the existing controls and rate the risk:

  • S comes from the effects linked to the mode (the highest), from table D1.
  • O is rated on the cause, taking into account the prevention controls (what keeps the cause from occurring: calculations, design rules, reviews, approved components, coding rules), from table D2. Note: in HW TCA you used table P2 of the process FMEA; the DFMEA uses D2, the design table.
  • D is rated on the detection controls (the test or analysis that finds the cause or the mode before release), from table D3. D depends on how proven the method is and on how early it is done: a good test, done after tooling release, still has D 5–7.
  • AP (action priority) follows from S, O and D, from the AP table: H means action needed, M action recommended, L action optional. We do not use RPN = S × O × D.

Optimization (step 6)

Where AP is H or M you propose actions, in the revised state. An action has a type, a text, a responsible person, a due date and a status. Type P (prevention) changes the design and lowers O; type D (detection) adds or moves a test and lowers D. After the actions you rate the new O and the new D; the revised AP shows whether the risk went down.

  • When an action is done, you choose its result: PASS or FAIL.
  • FAIL means the test found the failure: the detection worked, but the failure exists. Add a corrective design action (P) and re-rate O. A better test alone does not lower the risk.
  • If after the revision the risk is still H or M, propose an additional action (P, which lowers O, or D, which lowers D) and justify the residual risk. At S 9 with O 4–5, D 2–4 leaves AP H: in practice only prevention lowers the risk.

The link with testing

FMEA and testing work together. The detection controls in the DFMEA are in fact the tests in the design verification and validation plan: the EMC test, the measurements on the prototype, the requirements-based software tests. When a test FAILs, the result goes back into the FMEA. The previous labs cover the test side of the same elements:

  • HW TCA: the same components, but the causes are manufacturing causes (PFMEA), and the question is whether AOI, AXI, ICT and FT catch the defect before the board leaves the plant.
  • SW TCA: shows how complete the software tests used as detection in the SW DFMEA are. A low D on „Requirements-based testing” only makes sense if the tests cover the code and the boundaries.

Homework (the report)

At home you do the analysis on your own variant, for the HW signal and for the SW function. The steps are the ones from the lab, on F0:

  1. in the app press „Start over”, then load your variant (F1–F6), with your name;
  2. for HW and for SW: redo exercises 4, 6, 8 and 9 (one function per element, at most 3 modes, 3 effects and 2 causes per mode);
  3. on every cause choose the culprit (the component in HW, the requirement for SW causes in the requirements category);
  4. for AP H or M propose actions with a responsible person and a due date, and rate the new O and D; if the risk is still H or M, propose the additional action and justify the residual risk;
  5. write the conclusions, check the „Checks” list of each analysis, then, in the Report tab, press „Send report”. By sending it you agree that the data in the report may be used for a case study; your name is not used anywhere, only the results from the app. After sending, an anonymous 5-question questionnaire about the app and the exercise appears; you can fill it in or close it with „Not now”. If the sending is not confirmed, download the report (.html) and keep it.
